In 2001, Elon Musk had an idea to launch a greenhouse to Mars, so he went to Moscow trying to buy a refurbished ICBM from the Russians.

They said no, and on the flight home, he decided to make the rocket himself, and make it reusable.

Here's how a reusable rocket works.

Once it launches, the booster, bottom part, separates from the upper stage, and while the payload, stuff on it, continues on its course, the booster flips itself around using nitrogen thrusters, going further up and then back down again, burning the engines two more times to very slowly re-enter and touchdown vertically on a drone-operated ship in the ocean.

It's all autonomous.

The rocket lands itself.

It took 14 years to perfect this technology, but now each booster has 20 or so uses before repairs.

Reusing rockets is way cheaper.

NASA's SLS costs about $1.5 billion per launch.
